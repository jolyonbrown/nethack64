What it means for the C128 link (docs/data/wholegame-c128-fit-2026-11-22.txt): fc3 left the
resident side 2,050 B over with Oscar64's 3,434 B stack and no heap at all, so a near heap of
11-24 KB cannot come from squeezing; the monsters and objects go far, as ADR-0009 point 7
decided ("mons[], objects[] and the heap first"). What stays near is small: the windows,
the message history, the quest index, the special levels' list, about 3 KB. A block larger
than a page is the open case: a shopkeeper (the bill, about 2.3 KB on a C64, and Minetown has
up to five) and a vault guard (the fake corridor, 101 entries of 3 B). The far-spaces fork
translates each access to a struct larger than a page by itself, so such a block can lie
across far pages; but its entries (struct bill_x, struct fakecorridor) are far types of their
own, which the fork translates once per entry, so they must not cross a page: padded to a
power of two and the block page-aligned, as mons[] and objects[] were (ADR-0009 point 5).
