The C128 game at 1 MHz (fc50) and at 2 MHz with each REU DMA at 1 MHz (fc51), 2026-11-27.
VICE 3.7.1 x128 at real speed (no warp), Magic Desk 128 + 4 MB REU, a fresh REU image each.
Wall-clock seconds; the replay's keys go through tools/vice.py, which polls the screen
(about 0.7 s a look, the same for both builds), so its times include that overhead.

                                         fc50 (1 MHz)   fc51 (2 MHz)   ratio
launch to "Who are you?"                     15.4           15.4        1.00
the last role key to the first "--More--"    80.7           61.9        1.30
  (u_init and the first level's creation)
the replay to Dlvl 2 (about 400 turns)      652.7          576.8        1.13

The replay's last screen is the same for both builds (diffed).

Why not 2x: if T = C/k + D (C the CPU's share at 1 MHz, D what the clock does not change),
level creation gives C = 37.6 s and D = 43.1 s. Counters read through the binary monitor
on fc51 (nh_fetches: REU bank loads into the window, 16 KB each, about 16.6 ms at 1 MHz;
nh_faults / nh_writebacks: far pages):

                                 nh_fetches   nh_faults   nh_writebacks
at "Who are you?"                      534          17            17
before the last role key              2091         168           168
at the first "--More--"               3772        3733          3733

1,681 bank loads while the first level is made: about 28 s of DMA, two thirds of D. The
character menus cost 1,557 more. Every fault also wrote a page back (faults = writebacks).
Next for speed: the bank plan for level creation and the menus (which functions make the
window reload), and whether a clean far page needs writing back.
